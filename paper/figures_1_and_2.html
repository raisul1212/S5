Figure 1

Mambino layer architecture: main state-space model with a forward-only predictor branch and additive predictive-coding feedback

main SSM path predictor branch PC surprise + W_ε feedback output gate

Figure 1. One Mambino layer. Input x[t] feeds two parallel scans: the main SSM with parameters Λ̄, B̄, C̃, and a forward-only predictor SSM with independent parameters Λ_s, B_s, C_s. The predictor's output pred[t] subtracts from the input to form a predictive-coding surprise ε[t] = x[t] − pred[t], which is projected through the additive feedback matrix W̄_ε and summed with the main SSM's input projection. The main scan is bidirectional — its forward and backward pass state trajectories are concatenated along local_P before the output projection C̃. The final half_glu2 gate can be full-rank (Dense(H, H), Corner 1 setting) or rank-r low-rank factorised (Dense(H, r)∘Dense(r, H), Corner 3′ uses r = 40). Mambino Configs 4 and 5 use activation_fn=gelu, which bypasses the gate entirely.

Figure 2

Chip block topologies compared: pipelined vs sequential-layer streaming inference at 22 nm

SRAM / on-chip memory digital compute (MAC / regfile) SSM layer stage

Figure 2. Two chip topologies that differ only in activation SRAM depth and layer-pipeline width. The pipelined chip (left) keeps a 4.2 MB activation SRAM buffering the forward + backward pass streams of all eight Mambino layers concurrently and pipelines them at one timestep per stage per cycle, maximising throughput. The sequential chip (right) collapses to a single reused layer stage with a 512 KB activation SRAM buffering one layer at a time, cutting die area by ~4× and per-inference energy by ~3× at the cost of an ~8× longer latency. State SRAM sizing follows the streaming formula local_P × L × n_layers × 1 under the pipelined topology and local_P × L × 1 × 1 under the sequential topology (§11 of the master document). Weight SRAM, MAC array, and register file are identical across topologies. All PPAC totals are Accelergy 0.4 + CACTI + NeuroSim at 22 nm INT8, 1 GHz.
